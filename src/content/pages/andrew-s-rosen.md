Andrew Rosen is an Assistant Professor of Chemical and Biological Engineering at Princeton University, where he started his faculty career in 2024. In this role, he leads the Rosen Research Group, which uses quantum-mechanical simulations and machine learning to uncover the fundamental mechanisms governing reactivity and stability in solid-state materials. As quantum-chemical engineers, the group is particularly interested in understanding how exotic electronic structure properties can lead to materials with unprecedented chemical reactivity. In tandem, his group develops and applies computational methods that make it possible to go from electrons to complex processes describing the stability and decomposition mechanisms of solid-state materials. Andrew is particularly passionate about open-source science and is a developer or maintainer for many widely adopted software packages and databases in the computational materials science community.

Andrew has received numerous awards and honors, including being named an AIChE 35 Under 35 awardee, an Air Force Office of Scientific Research Young Investigator Program awardee, and a Breakthrough Energy Explorer Fellow. In addition, Andrew has received multiple Commendations for Outstanding Teaching while at Princeton. Andrew earned his B.S. in Chemical Engineering from Tufts University and his Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering from Northwestern University. Prior to starting his faculty career, he was a Miller Research Fellow in the Department of Materials Science and Engineering at the University of California, Berkeley.
