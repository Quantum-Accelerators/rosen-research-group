title: "Research"
intro: "We combine quantum chemistry, large-scale simulation, and machine learning to study reactivity and stability in solid-state materials."
approach: "Our research is organized around two overarching questions at the intersection of chemical engineering and materials science."
questions:
  - "What new reaction chemistry becomes possible in materials with exotic electronic configurations?"
  - "What governs the stability of a material and the series of reactions that lead to its decomposition?"
crosscutting: "These questions are unified by a crosscutting effort to develop the large-scale datasets, benchmarks, and open-source software infrastructure needed to make machine-learned interatomic potentials more accurate, reliable, and broadly useful across computational materials science."
areas:
  - label: "Research area 01"
    title: "Electronic structure beyond the atom."
    paragraphs:
      - "We use quantum-mechanical simulations and machine learning to understand how unusual electronic configurations in solid-state materials can give rise to fundamentally new forms of chemical reactivity."
      - "By studying electronic behavior that extends beyond conventional atom-centered chemical intuition, we aim to identify new reaction pathways and connect electronic dynamics with the atomic-scale processes that govern materials chemistry."

    title: "Stability and decomposition under realistic conditions."
    paragraphs:
      - "We develop and apply computational methods to determine what governs the stability of solid-state materials and to uncover the reaction mechanisms that drive their decomposition when they are pushed beyond their limits."
      - "Our work combines quantum-mechanical calculations, molecular dynamics, machine-learned interatomic potentials, and free-energy methods to study metastability, finite-temperature behavior, and material transformations across otherwise inaccessible length and time scales."
